# Linocut Bold

## Intent

Convert one photograph into a forceful relief-print poster built from carved marks and strong silhouettes. Preserve identity, gesture, important objects, and narrative direction while simplifying tonal detail into printable black-and-paper decisions.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas.
- Create one dominant silhouette or portrait occupying roughly 50–75% of the poster.
- Preserve recognizable facial structure, pose, clothing outline, and essential object shapes.
- Simplify the environment into a small number of carved planes, directional marks, or framing shapes.
- Use negative carving to separate edges and focal features. Keep hands, eyes, and important contours readable.

## Print language

- Use one to three flat relief inks plus the paper color.
- Build tone from carved hatching, gouged curves, chipped edges, and solid ink masses; do not use photographic halftone dots.
- Allow slight ink pressure variation and paper tooth while keeping the main silhouette crisp.
- Choose ink colors from the source or use black with one strong accent. Avoid smooth gradients.

## Typography

- Use a short supplied title or a neutral visible-subject label.
- Use bold block lettering that appears cut or printed with the same relief process.
- Never invent campaign slogans, political affiliations, event names, dates, prices, signatures, or printer marks.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is one flat 3:4 relief-print poster.
- Carved marks, not halftone or watercolor texture, create the image.
- The subject remains recognizable at poster-viewing distance.
- The palette stays within one to three inks plus paper.
- Text is sparse, legible, and factually safe.

## Avoid

Risograph halftones, smooth vector tracing, photographic grayscale, scratchboard over-detail, random distressed overlays, unreadable carving across faces, fake political slogans, copied activist graphics, ornate wood frames, glossy mockups, and more than three inks.
