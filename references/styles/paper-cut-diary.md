# Paper-Cut Diary

## Intent

Create a warm personal keepsake poster from one photograph. The source appears as a faithful photo window surrounded by restrained cut-paper shapes, tape, small drawn marks, and optional handwritten notes. The result should feel crafted and intimate, not childish or scrapbook-busy.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 4:5 vertical canvas.
- Keep one faithful photo window occupying roughly 45–65% of the canvas. It may have a simple rectangular, softly torn, or rounded paper edge.
- Preserve people, pets, food, objects, identity, facial features, proportions, clothing, and relationships.
- Use no more than five supporting paper shapes derived from visible colors or recognizable details in the photograph.
- Add up to two small tape pieces, thread marks, stamps, or pencil gestures to anchor the composition.
- Leave at least one generous area of untouched paper so the design can breathe.

## Material and color

- Use warm matte paper, light fibers, subtle shadows between layers, and hand-cut edges.
- Extract three to five colors from the photograph and soften them slightly for paper stock.
- Use shallow physical layering only. Keep the result flat enough to read as a scanned handmade poster rather than a deep 3D diorama.
- Allow tiny imperfections, but keep faces and the main photo free of stains, tears, or tape.

## Typography

- Use a short user-provided note, place, name, or date when supplied.
- If no text is supplied, use a neutral visible subject label or omit writing.
- Handwriting should be small, natural, and secondary. Pair it with at most one plain sans-serif caption.
- Never invent names, relationships, locations, dates, or personal memories.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is one flat 4:5 poster, not an open notebook or desk mockup.
- The original photo remains the emotional anchor and its subjects are unchanged.
- Craft elements are limited, tactile, and derived from the source palette.
- Negative space remains visible; the poster is not crowded.
- Any personal text is supplied by the user or visibly supported.

## Avoid

Childish clip art, excessive stickers, kawaii characters, glitter, glossy plastic, deep 3D shadow boxes, busy scrapbook pages, fake personal facts, distorted faces, covering eyes with decorations, and unrelated decorative objects.
