# Risograph Pulse

## Intent

Reinterpret one photograph as an energetic small-edition print poster. Recognition should survive a deliberately limited ink palette, coarse halftones, rough paper, and slight registration drift.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas.
- Crop boldly around the main subject while preserving identity, silhouette, pose, and essential objects.
- Use one dominant image field plus one strong title zone. A narrow border or off-center frame may expose the paper stock.
- Simplify midtone detail into screen-printed masses. Keep the eyes, face, key gesture, or defining object readable.
- Do not turn the poster into a multi-image concert collage unless the user explicitly asks.

## Ink and material

- Use exactly two or three simulated spot inks plus the paper color.
- Choose inks from the source when possible. Strong combinations include fluorescent pink with deep blue, red with black, or teal with warm orange, but do not force these combinations when they fight the photo.
- Use visible halftone dots, grain, dry ink edges, and slight registration offsets of roughly 1–3 mm.
- Keep misregistration controlled: it should create pulse and texture without producing a second face or unreadable subject.
- Use uncoated off-white, recycled gray, pale cream, or another believable paper stock.

## Typography

- Use a large, compact title supplied by the user or a short neutral label supported by the subject.
- Use one bold sans-serif or block display family, with optional small monospaced metadata.
- Let type share the ink system and participate in overprint.
- Never invent venue names, ticket details, performer names, sponsors, dates, or prices.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is one flat 3:4 poster on believable paper.
- Only two or three ink colors are visible, excluding the paper.
- Halftone and registration texture is clear but the subject remains identifiable.
- The title is readable and belongs to the print system.
- No unsupported event information appears.

## Avoid

Full-spectrum color, smooth digital gradients, glossy surfaces, perfect vector cleanliness, extreme glitch effects, illegible overprint, photocopy mud, fake folds, torn-poster mockups, and commercial sticker packs.
