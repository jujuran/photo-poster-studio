# Swiss Photo Grid

## Intent

Turn one photograph into a disciplined modernist poster. Combine a faithful photographic field with an asymmetric grid, large geometric color areas, and precise typography. The design should feel authored and architectural, not like a generic social-media template.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 4:5 vertical canvas.
- Build the layout on a visible but unprinted modular grid with strong alignment and generous margins.
- Use one primary photo crop occupying roughly 55–75% of the canvas. A second detail crop from the same source is optional, but do not duplicate faces or imply a second event.
- Preserve identity, proportions, product shape, architecture, and important visual relationships.
- Add no more than three geometric elements: solid rectangles, rules, circles, or bars. Each must serve alignment, contrast, or hierarchy.
- Keep intentional negative space. Do not fill every grid cell.

## Color

- Keep the photograph natural or lightly desaturated.
- Select one accent color from the source and pair it with black, white, warm gray, or off-white.
- A second accent is allowed only when it improves information hierarchy.
- Favor flat, exact color fields. Avoid gradients, glows, bevels, shadows, and glossy effects.

## Typography

- Use a neutral grotesk or neo-grotesk sans-serif with clear size contrast.
- Use user-provided wording when available. Otherwise use one neutral subject label supported by the image.
- Limit the composition to a headline of at most five words plus one small metadata line.
- Align type rigorously to the grid. Rotated text is allowed once, at 90 degrees, when it improves structure.
- Never invent brand claims, prices, dates, addresses, event details, or logos.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is one flat 4:5 poster.
- The grid feels intentional through consistent edges and spacing.
- The source remains recognizable and undistorted.
- There are no more than three geometric elements and two accent colors.
- Typography is legible, sparse, and aligned rather than decorative.

## Avoid

Template-like card layouts, dashboard UI, faux-Bauhaus clutter, random circles, excessive tiny text, multiple font families, drop shadows, 3D type, gradients, stickers, decorative icons, and arbitrary data visualization.
