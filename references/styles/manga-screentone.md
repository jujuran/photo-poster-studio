# Manga Screentone

## Intent

Translate one photograph into an original black-and-white manga poster. Preserve identity, expression, pose, clothing, essential objects, and scene direction while increasing graphic drama through ink contours, selective screentones, and controlled motion.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas.
- Build one dominant panel-like composition, not a full comic page, unless the user explicitly asks for multiple panels.
- Keep the main face or gesture as the focal point. Use crop, diagonal framing, or foreground shapes to create energy without changing the action.
- Add speed lines, impact marks, or expressive background strokes only when the source supports movement or tension.
- Do not introduce new characters, costumes, weapons, powers, or story events.

## Ink and tone

- Use clean black ink, white paper, and two or three screentone densities.
- Preserve facial anchors and body proportions through confident contour lines and economical shadow masses.
- Use hatching for form and screentone for value separation. Keep moire under control and avoid muddy gray coverage.
- One small accent color is optional only when requested.

## Typography

- Use user-provided wording for titles, dialogue, or sound effects.
- Without supplied copy, use a short neutral title supported by the scene or omit lettering.
- Keep sound effects original and semantically appropriate. Never fabricate dialogue, names, episode numbers, publisher marks, or credits.
- Do not copy a named manga creator, existing character design, franchise logo, or recognizable series layout.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is one flat 3:4 black-and-white poster.
- The source person or subject remains recognizable and correctly proportioned.
- Screentones are intentional and do not obscure eyes, hands, or defining objects.
- Motion devices match the photographed action rather than inventing a new event.
- No unsupported dialogue, credits, or franchise elements appear.

## Avoid

Generic anime color rendering, copied character costumes, oversized sparkling eyes that erase identity, random Japanese text, excessive panel grids, muddy screentones, moire, duplicate faces, invented violence, fake publisher logos, and unreadable sound effects.
