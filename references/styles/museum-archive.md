# Museum Archive

## Intent

Present one photograph as a carefully catalogued visual artifact. Use exhibition-scale margins, quiet institutional typography, and subtle archival devices to create authority without pretending the image belongs to a real collection.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas.
- Place one undistorted photograph within generous margins, occupying roughly 50–70% of the canvas.
- Preserve the source crop when it is compositionally strong. If recropping is necessary, protect the main subject, edges of artworks or objects, and architectural geometry.
- Use one alignment system: centered plate, left-aligned catalog page, or bottom-weighted exhibition poster. Do not mix systems.
- Optional archival devices are limited to two: a fine rule, plate number, crop mark, accession-like placeholder, or small color chip.
- Do not add seals, institutional logos, signatures, provenance marks, or authenticity stamps.

## Color and material

- Use warm white, museum gray, charcoal, or a muted color sampled from the photograph.
- Keep the image natural, with restrained contrast and accurate material color.
- Add only subtle paper tooth or print grain. The result should feel like a fine catalog page or exhibition announcement.
- Avoid artificial aging unless the user explicitly requests it.

## Typography

- Use an elegant serif or neutral sans-serif, with museum-like hierarchy and wide spacing.
- Use user-provided title, creator, date, medium, or location when available.
- Without supplied metadata, use only a neutral title supported by the visible subject and a non-factual label such as `PLATE 01`.
- Never invent artist names, collection names, accession numbers, provenance, dates, dimensions, venues, or curatorial claims.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is one flat 3:4 exhibition poster or catalog plate.
- The photograph is undistorted, quiet, and given generous space.
- Metadata is sparse, aligned, and factually safe.
- No real institution, collection, seal, or provenance is implied without user-provided information.
- Archival devices support hierarchy rather than decoration.

## Avoid

Fake museum branding, fabricated provenance, faux-antique stains, distressed parchment, heavy sepia, crowded labels, excessive crop marks, certificate layouts, auction-house styling, ornate frames, gold foil, and luxury-brand mockups.
