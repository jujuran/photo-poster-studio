# Cinematic Story Poster

## Intent

Transform one source photograph into a dramatic but believable film-poster image. Preserve the photographed people, objects, setting, and story; create cinematic emphasis through crop, light, depth, color, and restrained typography rather than invented spectacle.

## Format and composition

- Use a strict 2:3 vertical canvas.
- Keep one clear visual protagonist. Preserve identity, facial features, body proportions, pose, clothing, and important objects.
- Let the original environment remain recognizable. Extend only plausible sky, ground, walls, foliage, haze, or ambient background when the vertical crop needs space.
- Build depth with foreground shadow or texture, a readable midground subject, and softly receding atmosphere.
- Reserve one calm area for a short title, normally near the top or bottom. Do not cover a face or important action.
- Do not add extra people, vehicles, landmarks, weather events, explosions, or fantasy elements unless the user asks for them.

## Light and color

- Derive the grade from the source lighting. Strengthen direction and separation without changing time of day unless requested.
- Use one coherent cinematic color relationship, such as warm light against cool shadow or muted earth against deep blue.
- Allow subtle bloom, halation, haze, film grain, and controlled vignette.
- Retain natural skin color and material texture. Avoid plastic skin, crushed blacks, neon overload, and generic orange-teal grading.

## Typography

- Use one short user-provided title when available, plus at most one small subtitle or date supplied by the user.
- If no wording is supplied, use a neutral two- or three-word title supported by the visible scene, or omit text.
- Favor elegant condensed sans-serif, restrained serif, or understated film-title lettering.
- Never invent actor names, director credits, festival laurels, ratings, release dates, or production logos.

## Acceptance checks

- The result is a single flat 2:3 poster, not a mockup.
- The subject remains unmistakably the same person, object, or place as the source.
- Cinematic mood comes from photographic treatment, not fabricated plot elements.
- Title placement is legible, restrained, and does not obstruct the subject.
- No unsupported factual credits appear.

## Avoid

Cheap action-movie effects, artificial lens flares, fantasy composites, duplicate subjects, celebrity resemblance drift, over-smoothed faces, excessive copy, fake award marks, borders resembling a streaming thumbnail, or a busy montage.
